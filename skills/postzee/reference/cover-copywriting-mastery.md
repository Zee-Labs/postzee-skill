# Cover Copywriting Mastery

A cover headline is an **ad**. It has 0.4 seconds to earn the swipe. This doc teaches the agent to write cover copy like a senior copywriter at a creative agency — specific, committed, anti-cliché.

> Sibling doc: `cover-design-mastery.md` — how the cover *looks*. Read both before designing any cover.
> Engine for headline generation discipline: `carousel-headline-engine.md` — keeps the rejection checklist, coverage rule, and pattern/trigger lift tables.

## 1. The 6 hook patterns

Every great cover collapses to one of 6 hook patterns. Use them to *generate*, not as a finished menu — winners come from combining a pattern with a sharp specific.

### 1.1 Anti-claim — say what nobody is saying

Reverse the consensus. If the room agrees, oppose. If everyone says X, write the version that says "X is the problem."

**Examples**:
- "A morte do gosto pessoal."  *(everyone celebrates AI; you mourn what it kills)*
- "Ninguém precisa de outro curso."  *(in a flooded course market)*
- "Parem de tentar viralizar."  *(in a feed obsessed with reach)*

**Use when**: the topic is saturated. Anti-claim cuts through repetition.
**Risk**: pure contrarianism without a real argument inside. The slides must *earn* the anti-claim.

### 1.2 Pair + outcome — two things, one consequence

Two specific nouns, then what their collision produces. Causal, not poetic.

**Examples**:
- "Algoritmo + ansiedade = sua linha do tempo."
- "Pressa + medo = pivôs ruins."
- "Café + 4h de sono = a estratégia que matou meu Q1."

**Use when**: the argument needs a clear cause→effect. Pair forces specificity.
**Risk**: turning it into a math equation that reads cold. Keep the right side punchy and human.

### 1.3 Pessoal-confessional — the part nobody admits

First-person admission of something the audience secretly recognizes. Vulnerable, specific, not therapy-speak.

**Examples**:
- "Eu chorei na consulta."
- "Demitiram meu melhor cliente. Eu fiquei aliviado."
- "Quase fechei a empresa em fevereiro. Ninguém soube."

**Use when**: the audience is founders, leaders, creatives — people who present strength publicly. Confession trades reach for resonance.
**Risk**: oversharing without payoff. The slides must convert the confession into useful insight.
**Don't fake**: if it's not true, the audience smells it. Better to use 1.1 or 1.2.

### 1.4 Proof-by-number — the single specific that earns trust

A number so specific it can't be invented. Not "lots" or "many" — exactly *that*.

**Examples**:
- "47% dos meus posts não chegaram a ninguém."
- "R$ 12.400 em ads. 3 vendas."
- "Demorou 19 meses pra eu entender."

**Use when**: the topic is measurable and you have a real number. Proof-by-number kills "isn't this obvious?" doubt instantly.
**Risk**: round numbers (50%, 100, 10x) read as fabricated. Real data is 47%, 12.400, 19. Specificity is the entire mechanism.
**Don't fake**: if you don't have the number, write a different headline. Made-up specifics destroy trust on detection.

### 1.5 Slow-burn hook — the half-statement

A clause that's almost a sentence. Leaves the punchline in the slides. Used in literary/editorial covers.

**Examples**:
- "O que descobri depois de demitir."
- "A pergunta que ninguém faz em onboarding."
- "Onde eu errei sobre conteúdo."

**Use when**: the carousel is a story, not an argument. Slow-burn invites reading; argument-style headlines invite agreement.
**Risk**: too vague = thumb-stop fails. The half must hint at a *specific* second half, not "5 lessons about leadership."

### 1.6 Direct provocation — say the harsh thing out loud

Confrontational, named, no euphemism. Calls something or someone what it is.

**Examples**:
- "Seu CEO está mentindo sobre IA."
- "Marketing não é cultura."
- "Você não está cansado. Está sem propósito."

**Use when**: the audience expects polite framing and you want to break the pattern. Direct provocation works because everyone else is hedging.
**Risk**: provocation without substance = troll. The slides must *earn* the harshness with argument or evidence.
**Don't use**: for B2B sales-pipeline content. The audience there punishes direct provocation.

## 2. The specificity rule

**Every word in a cover headline must be doing work.** If a word can be removed without changing meaning, it's slop. If a word could be replaced by a more specific word, replace it.

| Slop | Specific | Why specific wins |
|---|---|---|
| "muitas empresas" | "47% das startups SaaS" | quantified, scoped |
| "investir em conteúdo" | "postar 3× por semana" | actionable, concrete |
| "o futuro do trabalho" | "trabalho híbrido em 2026" | dated, narrowed |
| "leadership" | "demitir quem você gosta" | named the actual hard part |
| "comunicação eficaz" | "ligar quando podia ter mandado email" | scene, not concept |

**Test**: if the headline could appear on a LinkedIn ghostwriter's post, it's too generic. Rewrite until it could *only* belong to this carousel.

## 3. Anti-platitudes — the forbidden list

These phrases mark amateur copy. Generate, then strike. If your draft includes any, rewrite.

- ❌ "O futuro de [X]" / "The future of [X]"
- ❌ "Por que [X] está mudando tudo" / "Why [X] changes everything"
- ❌ "5 lições que aprendi sobre [X]" / "5 lessons about [X]"
- ❌ "O segredo de [X] que ninguém te conta"
- ❌ "Você precisa saber isso sobre [X]"
- ❌ "Game changer" / "Disruptive" / "Revolucionário"
- ❌ "Hack" / "Truque" / "Estratégia secreta"
- ❌ "Em [N] passos simples"
- ❌ Any sentence that ends with "..." as suspense
- ❌ Any rhetorical question that the audience can answer with "yes" (e.g. "Você quer crescer no LinkedIn?")

**Why these fail**: they're patterns the algorithm trained the audience to skip. Eyes have learned to filter them faster than they're read.

## 4. Copy + visual synergy

Cover copy and cover design are one piece, not two. They reinforce or they fight.

### 4.1 The voice → pattern map

| Copy voice | Best design patterns | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Anti-claim (1.1) | 1.5 full-bleed text, 1.6 text-on-color-block | the *idea* is the hero — image should retreat |
| Pair + outcome (1.2) | 1.1 lower-third, 1.6 text-on-color-block | structured copy wants structured layout |
| Pessoal-confessional (1.3) | 1.1 lower-third, 1.8 centered-on-negative-space, editorial serif | intimate copy wants quiet design |
| Proof-by-number (1.4) | 1.4 right-third, 1.6 text-on-color-block with the number isolated | number = visual element, give it room |
| Slow-burn hook (1.5) | 1.2 upper-third, 1.8 centered-on-negative-space, editorial serif | story tone wants newspaper/magazine vibe |
| Direct provocation (1.6) | 1.3/1.4 vertical thirds, 1.7 diagonal, display sans | loud copy, loud design |

### 4.2 The clash rule

A confessional headline in Druk Condensed Bold over a diagonal background is **wrong**. The copy whispers; the design screams. Match the volume.

A direct-provocation headline in Tiempos centered serif over soft negative space is **wrong**. The copy punches; the design apologizes. Match the volume.

When in doubt: read the headline out loud. If it sounds like a friend confessing, design quiet. If it sounds like a manifesto on a billboard, design loud.

### 4.3 Color synergy

- **Anti-claim, confession, slow-burn**: warm neutrals (off-white, cream, deep ink black). The point is *thoughtfulness*; saturated color undercuts it.
- **Pair + outcome, proof-by-number, direct provocation**: one accent color that pops against the photo. Cyan, magenta, yellow, neon green. Use the accent *only* on the part you want noticed (the number, the outcome word, the verb).

## 5. The 10→1 surface (winner-first)

The carousel headline engine (`carousel-headline-engine.md`) generates 10 candidates internally with full discipline (rejection checklist, coverage rule, pattern/trigger lift). The user sees **1**, defended.

```
✨ Capa mais forte:

   "A morte do gosto pessoal."

   Padrão: Anti-claim · 4 palavras · pattern Death/End (+119% lift)
   Por quê: nomeia o conflito sem revelar o ângulo. Quem
   sente o problema reconhece em 0.4s. Quem não sente, swipa.

   👉 "vai" / "boa"     — sigo com essa pra design da capa
      "outras"           — te mostro o top-3
      "todas"            — te mostro as 10 numeradas
```

**Why winner-first**:
- A senior copywriter doesn't present 10 options to a client — they present *the* recommendation and defend it.
- Menus dilute confidence. The user has to do the picking work the agent should have done.
- The discipline is internal; the deliverable is one bet.

**When to surface multiple**:
- User explicitly says "me dá 3 opções" or "quero comparar"
- User rejects the first winner and asks for alternatives
- Brief itself is ambiguous and the agent legitimately can't pick (rare — usually means asking one more clarifying question would have helped)

## 6. The 4-word ceiling (use, don't worship)

Short cover headlines outperform long ones in social feeds. 4 words is a strong default for cover slides; 6 is the upper bound; 8+ is rare and earned.

**Why short wins**:
- Feed view shows the cover as a thumbnail — long headlines wrap weirdly or get truncated
- Eye dwell time on a feed thumbnail is ~0.4s — 4 words fits
- Short forces specificity (you can't pad)

**When to break the rule**:
- Slow-burn hooks (1.5) often need a clause structure — "O que descobri depois de demitir" is 7 words and works
- Pair + outcome (1.2) is naturally longer — accept it
- Proof-by-number (1.4) with a long context: "47% dos meus posts não chegaram a ninguém" is 10 words because the specificity *is* the headline; cutting it kills it

**Never**: pad a 3-word headline to 4 with filler ("realmente", "agora", "hoje"). Better 3 strong words than 4 weak ones.

## 7. Writer's discipline before submit

Before surfacing the winner to the user, the agent runs this private check (silent, internal):

- [ ] Does any word in the headline appear in §3 anti-platitudes list?
- [ ] Could the headline appear on a LinkedIn ghostwriter's generic post? (If yes, kill.)
- [ ] Is there at least one specific (number, named noun, scene, confession)?
- [ ] Does the voice match the carousel's actual content? (Anti-claim winner promises an argument — do the slides deliver one?)
- [ ] Does the chosen design pattern (§4.1) synergize with the voice?
- [ ] Is the headline ≤6 words, or longer for a justifiable §6 reason?
- [ ] Did the rejection checklist from `carousel-headline-engine.md` §7 actually run, or did you skip it because the first draft "felt good"?

If any answer is "no", iterate before surfacing. The user trusts the recommendation because the discipline is invisible — undermine the discipline once and the trust is gone.
