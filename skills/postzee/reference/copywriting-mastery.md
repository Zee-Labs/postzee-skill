# Copywriting Mastery

This file is the agent's copywriter brain. It exists because **copy is what makes the difference between a polished design that gets scrolled past and a polished design that stops the scroll**. Editorial typography + composition give you the *chance* to be read; the words decide whether you actually are.

The agent must internalize this file before writing any headline, body, or caption — for carousels (via `carousel-mastery.md`), for single images (via `image-mastery.md`), or for post captions (via `captions-frameworks.md`). The lift table in `carousel-headline-engine.md` §2 quantifies WHICH patterns perform; this file teaches WHY they do, where they come from, and how to wield them.

The work synthesized here comes from the master copywriters whose books still sit on the shelves of every serious copy desk:
- **Dan Kennedy** — direct response; "The Ultimate Sales Letter"
- **Eugene Schwartz** — "Breakthrough Advertising"; awareness levels + mass desire
- **Joseph Sugarman** — 31 psychological triggers; "The Adweek Copywriting Handbook"
- **Gary Halbert** — "The Boron Letters"; starving crowd + writing to one person
- **Claude Hopkins** — "Scientific Advertising"; specificity over hype
- **David Ogilvy** — long copy works when it earns the next sentence
- **Eddie Shleyner (VeryGoodCopy)** — modern compression discipline
- **Harry Dry (Marketing Examples)** — "show, don't tell" applied to social media

Plus modern application from accounts that perform at the 1% of social media: `@gpt_academico`, brand teams at Linear / Notion / Vercel / Stripe, and Brazilian SaaS marketing leaders (Nubank, Pipefy, Resultados Digitais).

## 1. The 10 Inviolable Laws

These are the laws that show up across every master. They override stylistic preference. Break them and the copy doesn't work, no matter how clever it sounds.

### Law 1 — Specificity beats vagueness, always

**Source**: Sugarman, Halbert, Hopkins, Harry Dry. Universal.

**Rule**: Replace every abstract claim with a concrete fact. Numbers, names, places, dates, percentages.

| ❌ Weak (abstract) | ✅ Strong (specific) |
|---|---|
| "Our solution is much better" | "Cuts conversion-funnel friction by 14% in tests" |
| "Many users save time" | "Pipefy customers report 5.3 hours saved per week" |
| "Top researchers approve" | "Endorsed by 3 of the 5 CNPq commission heads" |
| "Recently updated" | "Updated 9 days ago — May 6, 2026" |
| "Very engaging" | "Top 1% IG engagement (8.2% rate vs 0.8% niche baseline)" |

The principle has neurological backing: specific information feels true; abstract information feels like marketing. The reader's BS detector fires on "many", "much", "very", "incredibly" — and shuts down on "14%", "5.3 hours", "9 days ago".

**When the specific number isn't available**: don't fabricate. Use a SPECIFIC observation instead. "I watched the team try this 4 times before it worked" beats "Many teams struggle". "Last quarter at the agency" beats "In recent times".

### Law 2 — One promise per piece

**Source**: Eddie Shleyner, Schwartz, Halbert.

**Rule**: Every post, every carousel, every caption defends ONE idea. Multiple ideas dilute the reader's emotional response.

```
❌ "Sci-Hub deu passo importantíssimo para democratizar a ciência E o CNPq mudou regras E os detectores de plágio agora funcionam"

✅ "Sci-Hub deu passo importantíssimo para democratizar a ciência."
   (Then save the CNPq + detectors as separate posts.)
```

A reader processes ONE emotion at a time. A post with three promises forces them to choose which to feel — and "choose" is what the algorithm hates. Single-promise content gets longer dwell time → better distribution.

**Test**: read your draft and ask "what's the ONE thing I want them to feel/know after reading this?". If there are two, split into two pieces.

### Law 3 — Get into the conversation already in the reader's head

**Source**: Eugene Schwartz. Often called "the most important rule in advertising."

**Rule**: Don't try to START a conversation with the reader — JOIN one they're already having with themselves.

```
The reader doesn't decide "I want to read about Sci-Hub today".
They wake up frustrated that their university doesn't subscribe to the
Elsevier journal they need.

The headline that wins: "Aquela frustração de não conseguir baixar
o paper que você precisa? Sci-Hub acabou de dar um passo importantíssimo."

The headline that doesn't: "Sci-Hub: o que é e por que importa?"
```

Practical method:
- List the 5 things your target audience is privately frustrated/curious/anxious about RIGHT NOW
- The headline of any post is one of those 5, named precisely
- Never explain the audience's situation to themselves — show them you UNDERSTAND it

The lift table in `carousel-headline-engine.md` §2 ranks regional/cultural anchor at +155% — exactly because regional content joins a conversation locals are already having.

### Law 4 — Pattern interrupt in the first 5 words

**Source**: Sugarman, Kennedy. Reinforced by every viral SM analysis.

**Rule**: The first 5 words must STOP the scroll. Not entice — STOP. A scroll is a momentum; words have to break momentum.

Top-performing patterns observed at scale:

| Pattern | Effect | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Single-word + period | Creates pause | `FINALMENTE.` / `URGENTE.` / `PARE.` |
| Authority collapse | Triggers paradigm panic | `Caiu por terra.` / `Acabou.` |
| Hostile question | Forces engagement | `Você ainda usa X?` / `Quem aí também?` |
| Time-stamped news | Triggers fear of being late | `Esta semana,` / `A partir de hoje` |
| Direct command | Breaks passive scroll | `Olha isso.` / `Veja.` / `Não pule.` |
| Specific number | Promises payoff | `78% das marcas erram aqui.` |
| Subverted expectation | Curiosity | `Definiram os melhores X. Será?` |

The 5-word window matters because mobile feeds typically show ~5-7 words before truncating. If word 6+ is doing the work, you've already lost the algorithm.

### Law 5 — Authority signal in the first 5 words (when applicable)

**Source**: Kennedy, Halbert.

**Rule**: If your post has authority backing (institution, named source, percentage, study), surface it in the first 5 words. Authority compounds the pattern interrupt.

```
❌ "Olha o que aconteceu: o CNPq mudou suas regras sobre IA na pesquisa"
✅ "O CNPq mudou. Agora é obrigatório."
   (CNPq = authority; "mudou" = pattern interrupt; "obrigatório" = consequence)

❌ "Pesquisadores brasileiros estão usando ferramentas novas para detectar plágio"
✅ "MEC: 8 detectores de plágio que ainda funcionam em 2026."
   (MEC = authority + specific number + time frame)
```

When you DON'T have authority: borrow it from someone in your sector ("Como a Nubank fez X") or use a precise observation as proxy authority ("Em 3 reuniões esta semana, ouvi isto:").

### Law 6 — Write to ONE person, never to "everyone"

**Source**: Halbert. Every copywriting book agrees.

**Rule**: The reader is one person, alone, with the phone. They are not "your audience" or "users" or "people". Write as if you're sending a message to that specific person.

```
❌ "Para os profissionais que valorizam X, Y e Z..."
✅ "Você que tá há 3 anos tentando fazer Y dar resultado..."

❌ "Muitas pessoas têm dificuldade com isto"
✅ "Se você acabou de bater a cabeça com isto pela 4ª vez essa semana"
```

The most engaging posts read like a DM to one specific person who happens to be everyone in your niche. The audience EXPANDS the more specifically you write — because reading "you" with precise context, each reader privately thinks "this is for me".

`@gpt_academico` example: "Se você não é orientador nem revisor, pode pular essa publicação." This excludes 90% of Instagram users — and gets 53k views because the 10% who ARE in that category feel uniquely addressed.

### Law 7 — Slippery slope construction

**Source**: Sugarman's "the only purpose of any sentence is to make the reader read the next one".

**Rule**: Each sentence creates curiosity tension that the next sentence resolves — while opening new tension. The reader can't stop mid-paragraph.

Anatomy:

```
Sentence 1: claim with implicit question.
   "A internet da sua casa é mais rápida que a do escritório."
   [Reader's mental question: "wait, is that true?"]

Sentence 2: resolves Q1, opens Q2.
   "A do seu escritório é mais rápida que a do banco que cuida do seu salário."
   [Resolves Q1 affirmatively; opens "how is that possible?"]

Sentence 3: resolves Q2, opens Q3 OR delivers the payload.
   "Ninguém está dizendo isso em voz alta."
   [Resolves Q2 with "and here's why this matters"]
```

**Common failure**: writing each sentence as a complete thought. Each "thought" becomes a stop sign. Instead, write each sentence as a SETUP for the next.

### Law 8 — Show, don't tell

**Source**: Harry Dry (modernized from classic fiction-writing principle). Hopkins applied it to advertising.

**Rule**: Demonstrate the claim with a concrete example, screenshot, named source, or specific moment. Don't just state it.

| ❌ Tell | ✅ Show |
|---|---|
| "Lattes infinitos não significam mais nada" | "Lattes com infinitas publicações não querem dizer mais nada" + screenshot of a published paper showing 0 citations after 18 months |
| "Nosso produto é fácil de usar" | "3 cliques pra criar um post. Conta os cliques no vídeo." |
| "A IA está mudando o mercado" | "Em março de 2025, 1 marca por dia anunciava demissão. Em março de 2026, 12 por dia." |

The "show" form is more credible AND more memorable AND more shareable. The reader can repeat the specific to a friend; they can't repeat the abstract.

In carousel/single-image context: "show" often means including a screenshot, a quote with attribution, a chart, or a photograph that grounds the claim physically.

### Law 9 — The hook lives in the contradiction

**Source**: Schwartz's "mass desire mismatch". Modern viral analysis.

**Rule**: The best hooks reveal a contradiction between what the audience EXPECTS and what's actually true (or what you're claiming).

Templates:

```
Expectation: "Lots of publications = good researcher"
Contradiction: "Lattes com infinitas publicações não querem dizer mais nada."

Expectation: "Detectors of AI-generated text are getting better"
Contradiction: "Definiram os melhores detectores de plágio. Será?"

Expectation: "Internet is faster at the office"
Contradiction: "A internet da sua casa é mais rápida que a do escritório."

Expectation: "Premium products require premium effort"
Contradiction: "O resultado fica 15x melhor. Em 1 prompt."
```

Method:
1. Identify the dominant belief in your audience around the topic
2. Find the precise way it's wrong (or has an exception)
3. State the contradiction directly — no hedging

If you can't find a contradiction, you don't have a hook yet. You have a fact. Find the contradiction or find a different topic.

### Law 10 — Easy reading is hard writing

**Source**: Sugarman, Shleyner.

**Rule**: Cut every word that isn't load-bearing. The shorter the sentence, the more impact it has.

Editing process (apply in order):

1. **Replace adjectives with facts**. "Very fast" → "200ms". "Incredible improvement" → "from 30% to 78%".
2. **Cut hedges**. "I think", "perhaps", "maybe", "kind of", "sort of", "it seems".
3. **Cut filler connectives**. "In addition", "furthermore", "moreover", "therefore", "hence" — usually you can DELETE them, not replace.
4. **Cut decorative verbs**. "Engages with", "leverages", "utilizes" → "uses", "talks to", "applies".
5. **Cut "that"**. Most instances of "that" can be cut. "The thing that you want" → "what you want".
6. **Convert passive to active**. "X was done by Y" → "Y did X".
7. **Replace abstract nouns with verbs**. "The implementation of the solution" → "Implementing the solution".

Eddie Shleyner's compression heuristic: take any sentence. Try removing every word one at a time. If the sentence still works without the word — leave it out.

## 2. The 5 Awareness Levels (Eugene Schwartz)

Before writing any headline, the agent identifies WHERE the audience is. The wrong message at the wrong awareness level always fails.

| Level | Audience state | Headline approach | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 — Unaware** | Doesn't know they have the problem | Pattern interrupt + curiosity. Don't sell — observe. | "78% das marcas brasileiras não sabem disto." |
| **2 — Problem-aware** | Knows the problem, doesn't know solutions | Name the problem precisely + tease relief | "Aquela frustração de não conseguir baixar o paper que você precisa?" |
| **3 — Solution-aware** | Knows solutions exist, comparing | Compare or differentiate | "3 IA detectoras testadas. 2 falharam. 1 acertou 94%." |
| **4 — Product-aware** | Knows your product, hesitant | Authority + proof + risk-reversal | "Pipefy: 850 empresas brasileiras automatizaram este fluxo. Veja o caso da Movile." |
| **5 — Most aware** | Ready to act, needs trigger | Direct CTA + urgency | "Bolsas de pesquisa com 30% de reajuste. CNPq abriu inscrições. Prazo: 27 de maio." |

The agent's INFERENCE: from the user's brief, deduce the audience awareness level. Most carousels target **Level 1 or 2** (broader reach); most single-post CTAs target **Level 4 or 5** (conversion-focused).

When in doubt: target **Level 2** — it's the safest broad-reach default and the closest to where most social audiences actually are.

## 3. The 12 Hook Patterns Atlas

Expansion of the 5 patterns in `carousel-headline-engine.md` §3. The first 5 stay as-is (those have specific lift data); the next 7 add reach for cases the original 5 don't cover.

### 3.1 Death/End Pattern (legacy +119% lift)

```
"A morte do [X]."
"O fim do [X]."
"Por que [X] está morrendo (e o que vem depois)."
```

When: a practice, role, or genre is genuinely declining.

### 3.2 Generational Behavior (legacy +119%)

```
"Por que [Geração] está [comportamento inesperado]."
```

When: a cohort is doing something that contradicts the stereotype.

### 3.3 Investigation Framing (legacy +20-60%)

```
"Investigando por que [N em cada M] [comportamento]."
"O que descobri analisando [N] [casos / posts / dados]."
```

When: you have data the audience hasn't seen processed.

### 3.4 Brand-reveal Proxy (legacy)

```
"A operadora que ninguém estava prestando atenção [feito]."
"Existe uma marca brasileira que [conquista inesperada]."
```

When: highlighting an underdog success that audience will recognize after the reveal.

### 3.5 Two-Colon Structure (legacy)

```
"Por que [X]: e o que isso significa: pra [audience]."
```

When: you want to anchor a fact + interpret + apply. Rhythm-based.

### 3.6 Pattern Interrupt (NEW — viral 2026)

```
"FINALMENTE."
"URGENTE."
"PARE."
"OLHA ISSO."
"NÃO ROLA O FEED."
```

When: the post body has a payoff strong enough to justify the interrupt. NEVER use without a strong follow-up — the cost of pattern interrupt + weak content is the audience marking your account as clickbait.

### 3.7 Authority Collapse (NEW)

```
"Caiu por terra."
"Acabou a era de [X]."
"Não vale mais [tradição estabelecida]."
"[Authority figure] mudou de ideia."
```

When: a long-held assumption in the niche just got contradicted.

### 3.8 Specific Number Tease (NEW)

```
"15x melhor."
"78% das marcas erram aqui."
"3 em cada 5 [grupo] não sabem disto."
"De R$ 12k pra R$ 47k em 6 meses. Como."
```

When: you have a real specific number to anchor curiosity.

### 3.9 Question Subversion (NEW)

```
"Definiram os melhores X. Será?"
"Todo mundo fala em [tendência]. Mas isso serve pra você?"
"Mas a IA serve mesmo pra isso? Veja o que descobri."
```

When: a popular take is overconfident and you can show why.

### 3.10 Authority + Urgency (NEW — high conversion)

```
"O CNPq mudou. Agora é obrigatório."
"BNDES abriu chamada. Prazo: [date]."
"Anvisa publicou nova norma sobre [X]."
```

When: a real institution made a real change with a real deadline.

### 3.11 Honest Counterpoint (NEW)

```
"Antes que fique pra trás."
"O que ninguém comenta."
"Se você não é [excluded group], pode pular essa publicação."
"Vou ser honesto: a maioria não vai gostar."
```

When: you want to filter the audience to the people who genuinely need the message. Excluding signals authenticity.

### 3.12 Time-Stamped News (NEW)

```
"Esta semana,"
"A partir de hoje,"
"Em [exact date],"
"24 horas atrás,"
```

When: the content is genuinely time-bound. Always pair with a real date — fake urgency is detectable in 1 scroll.

**Pattern coverage rule**: across a 10-headline batch (see `carousel-headline-engine.md` §9), the agent must cover at least 3 of these 12 patterns. Don't repeat the same pattern across all variations.

## 4. The Hook Anatomy (single-image and slide-1 cover)

For single-image posts and carousel cover slides, the full hook is built from up to four components, in order:

```
[Pattern Interrupt] + [Authority Signal] + [Specific Number/Name] + [Promise/Question]
```

Worked examples:

```
FINALMENTE.  |  O Sci-Hub  |  dá UM passo importantíssimo  |  pra democratizar a ciência
[interrupt]    [authority]   [specific verb + degree]        [promise]

CAIU POR TERRA.  |  Lattes com infinitas publicações  |  não querem dizer mais nada  |  E você precisa saber disso AGORA
[collapse]         [specific subject]                   [reversal]                    [urgency]

Definiram  |  os melhores detectores de plágio  |  de 2026.  |  Será?
[setup]      [specific category]                  [time]      [question subversion]

O resultado fica 15x melhor.  |  Em 1 prompt.
[specific number]               [specific scope]

URGENTE.  |  Se você não é orientador nem revisor  |  pode pular essa publicação.
[interrupt]  [filter]                                [exclusion]
```

Not every hook needs all 4. The strongest hooks use 2-3 components, each loaded:

- **Slot 1 (Pattern Interrupt)** — optional; use when the audience needs a momentum break (top-of-funnel, cold audience)
- **Slot 2 (Authority Signal)** — use whenever truthful authority exists
- **Slot 3 (Specific Number/Name)** — almost always use; specificity is the universal multiplier
- **Slot 4 (Promise/Question)** — required; tells the reader what they'll get from continuing

## 5. Caption Architecture (4 templates)

Captions are what makes the post WORK on the algorithm. They're longer than headlines (50-500 words for IG/LinkedIn) and they carry the engagement signal (saves, comments, shares).

### 5.1 AIDA — Attention / Interest / Desire / Action

Best for: product/service posts at Awareness Level 4-5.

```
Attention:  [hook from §4]
Interest:   [one paragraph framing the problem in the reader's own language]
Desire:     [one paragraph showing the resolved state — emotion + outcome]
Action:     [explicit CTA — comment "X", link in bio, save the post]
```

### 5.2 PAS — Problem / Agitate / Solve

Best for: solution-aware audience (Level 3). High conversion.

```
Problem:    [Name the specific pain]
Agitate:    [Make the pain felt. 2-3 sentences showing the cost of not solving it]
Solve:      [Your specific resolution. Specific. Numbered. Concrete.]
```

The agitation paragraph is what separates good PAS from bad PAS. It must hurt. Without the agitation, the reader files the post under "interesting" and moves on. With it, they file under "I need to act on this".

### 5.3 BAB — Before / After / Bridge

Best for: transformations, case studies, before/after results.

```
Before:  [Reader's current state, with specific details]
After:   [Resolved state, also specific]
Bridge:  [The mechanism / product / decision that connects them]
```

### 5.4 Hook → Promise → Payoff → CTA (modern long-form)

Best for: educational posts at Awareness Level 1-3.

```
Hook:     [§4 anatomy — single line]
Promise:  [What this post will teach you in one sentence]
Payoff:   [3-7 lines of actual content — numbered list, named examples, specific steps]
CTA:      [Comment / save / share / link — pick ONE]
```

The single-CTA rule: do NOT mix CTAs. "Save this AND share AND comment AND click the link" → reader does none. Pick the ONE behavior that matters and ask for it precisely.

## 6. The Brazilian Voice

PT-BR copy has specific dynamics that don't translate from English playbooks. The agent internalizes these:

### 6.1 Tone register

The right register is **smart friend, not corporate professional**. Brazilian audiences are sensitive to anything that sounds like a polished press release — they tune it out in 2 seconds.

| ❌ Corporate | ✅ Smart-friend |
|---|---|
| "Apresentamos resultados superiores" | "É 15x melhor. Sério." |
| "Implementação eficiente de soluções" | "Resolveu o problema. Sem encheção." |
| "Estamos comprometidos com a excelência" | "A gente não publica se não funcionar." |
| "Convidamos você a conhecer" | "Vem ver isto antes que eu mude de assunto." |

### 6.2 Anglicism economics

Avoid inglesismo when a precise Portuguese word exists:

| ❌ Default English | ✅ Portuguese equivalent |
|---|---|
| "framework" | "estrutura" / "esquema" |
| "insight" | "sacada" / "percepção" |
| "mindset" | "mentalidade" / "cabeça" |
| "deliver" | "entregar" |
| "stakeholder" | "envolvido" / "interessado" (depending on context) |
| "leverage" | "usar" / "aproveitar" |
| "engagement" (when talking to BR audience) | "engajamento" — KEEP this one; it's lexicalized |
| "feedback" | "retorno" (often better) |

When the English word is more precise (e.g., "carrossel" works fine, but "post" is dominant in BR even though "publicação" exists) — keep the English. Don't translate for the sake of translating.

### 6.3 Regional anchors

The lift data (`carousel-headline-engine.md` §2): regional/cultural anchor gives **+155% engagement** vs baseline. Use it whenever the topic permits:

```
✅ "No Brasil, isso já vem acontecendo desde 2023."
✅ "Em São Paulo, 3 agências já mudaram a abordagem."
✅ "A geração brasileira que cresceu com discada agora cobra latência de 8ms."
✅ "Lula sancionou a lei na semana passada. E ninguém comentou."
```

The anchor doesn't have to be the headline subject — it just has to be present. "Como X funciona no contexto brasileiro" beats "How X works" even when the body is identical.

### 6.4 Cultural references that work

Use sparingly (1 per post, never more) and only when natural to the topic:

- **Carnaval / São João** — seasonal, only Jan-Mar / June-July
- **Política contemporânea** — only when the news IS the topic; never as cheap reference
- **Cultura pop BR** — Big Brother Brasil, novelas, hits da música (use what's CURRENTLY peaking, not nostálgia)
- **Linguagem regional** — "tá ligado" / "se pá" / "manja" — use when audience is clearly Geração Z/Alfa; avoid for B2B professional context
- **Memes BR atuais** — only the ones that haven't peaked yet (always check; meme half-life is ~3 weeks now)

### 6.5 Honest counterpoint as Brazilian voice signature

The "honest counterpoint" pattern (3.11 above) is particularly powerful in BR because the audience is hyperaware of marketing-speak. A line like:

```
"Vou ser honesto: esta abordagem só funciona pra quem já tem audiência mínima.
 Se você tá começando, melhor não tentar."
```

...DISQUALIFIES part of the audience. Counterintuitively, it makes the qualified audience trust you more — because the marketing instinct would be to claim universal applicability. Disqualifying = telling the truth = credibility.

## 7. Worked Examples — End-to-end Copy for Real Posts

### 7.1 Single-image post: educational + solution-aware

**Brief**: post about CNPq's new AI rules for academic research (announced May 2026). Audience: BR researchers/PhD students.

**Hook (4-component anatomy)**:
```
URGENTE.  O CNPq publicou novas regras de IA.  Agora é obrigatório declarar uso de IA em qualquer publicação científica.
```

**Caption (PAS, 130 words)**:
```
URGENTE. O CNPq publicou novas regras de IA. Agora é obrigatório declarar uso 
de IA em qualquer publicação científica.

Se você tá no meio de uma submissão pra revista — esta semana — pause.
Se você tá redigindo TCC, dissertação ou tese — atualize a metodologia.
Se você orienta — alerta o orientando antes que ele submeta.

A regra entra em vigor em 60 dias. Não declarar uso de IA = retratação 
automática + bloqueio de submissão por 24 meses.

A boa notícia: o template de declaração tem 4 linhas. Não é burocracia, 
é honestidade.

No próximo carrossel: o template exato + os 8 sinais que os revisores 
estão treinados a procurar.

Comenta "CNPQ" que mando o template direto pra ti.
```

Components present: Authority + Urgency + Specific Date + Filter + Honest Counterpoint + Single CTA. Every paragraph earns the next.

### 7.2 Carousel cover slide

**Brief**: 9-slide carousel "Por que sua carreira de pesquisa vai mudar até 2027".

**Cover hook (Two-Colon + Generational)**:
```
A geração que cresceu com discada: agora cobra latência de 8ms: e ninguém 
está dimensionando o impacto disso na pesquisa científica brasileira.
```

22 words. Within IC ceiling. Generational pattern + regional anchor + specific number. Rejection checklist clean.

### 7.3 Brand/product post (Notion-style minimalism)

**Brief**: Postzee announcing the v3.7 carousel feature to existing users.

**Hook + caption (Hook→Promise→Payoff→CTA, 95 words)**:
```
You write the words. Postzee renders the design.

Hoje liberamos o que mudou: carrosséis e imagens com qualidade de design 
editorial, criados por conversa.

Conversa com o Claude → preview no artifact (grátis, instantâneo) → 
aprova → renderiza pro feed.

Sem Canva. Sem Photoshop. Sem 12 abas no navegador.

Os primeiros 50 usuários a publicar um carrossel feito assim ganham 
o pacote de 1000 créditos. Comenta "VOU" e eu mando o link.
```

## 8. The Rejection Checklist (positive complement to carousel-editorial-filter §7)

Before sending copy to the user, the agent runs every line through these positive checks:

- [ ] **Specificity** — every claim has a fact, number, name, or date backing it
- [ ] **One promise** — only one idea is being defended
- [ ] **Conversation join** — the copy enters a conversation the audience is already having
- [ ] **First-5-words pattern interrupt** — checked
- [ ] **Audience-of-one voice** — written to one person, not "everyone"
- [ ] **Slippery slope** — each sentence sets up the next
- [ ] **Show, don't tell** — at least one concrete example/specific present
- [ ] **Contradiction** — the hook reveals something the audience didn't expect
- [ ] **Compression** — no adjectives without facts, no filler connectives, no hedge words
- [ ] **Single CTA** — one ask, not three

Fail any → rewrite the failing part. Don't ship until all 10 pass.
